1. INTRODUCTION & PHILOSOPHY OF PHYSICS-GUIDED EVALUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In standard machine learning, models are evaluated purely on data-driven metrics
such as Mean Squared Error (MSE) or Mean Absolute Error (MAE). If a neural network
outputs a smooth surface that minimizes average Euclidean distance to the training
labels, traditional ML marks it as a success.

However, in thermal modeling of semiconductor electronic chips, temperature fields
are governed by fundamental laws of continuum mechanics:
  - Anisotropic heat conduction (Fourier's Law) with temperature-dependent conductivity:
      kx(T) = kx_base * (T / T_ref)^(-n)
      ky(T) = ky_base * (T / T_ref)^(-n)
  - Energy conservation and steady-state heat divergence:
      d/dx( kx dT/dx ) + d/dy( ky dT/dy ) - (h_sink / d_chip)*(T - T_ambient) + Q_vol = 0
  - Boundary heat fluxes (Dirichlet fixed temperatures, Neumann adiabatic insulation,
    and Robin convective dissipation).

A purely data-driven model (such as a standard Fourier Neural Operator, FNO) can achieve
low error on average grid points but might predict unphysical heat sources, violate
boundary flux balances, or create energy out of nothing. Conversely, a Physics-Informed
Neural Network (PINN) incorporates physical PDE residuals directly into its loss function:
    L_total = w_data * L_data + w_pde * L_pde + w_bc * L_bc

This evaluation pipeline evaluates BOTH data accuracy and physics consistency across
all 800 held-out validation samples (819,200 grid cells). Crucially, the TEST SET (test.h5)
remains locked and was never accessed.


2. CORE EVALUATION METRICS: WHAT DO THEY MEAN?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. MEAN ABSOLUTE ERROR (MAE):
   - Formula: MAE = (1 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N |T_pred,i - T_true,i|
   - Units: Kelvin [K] (note: temperature differences have identical numerical
     magnitude in Celsius [°C]).
   - Meaning: The average magnitude of temperature prediction error across all chip pixels.
   - Why lower is better: Indicates typical deviation from the true temperature field.
   - Context: In electronic chips where temperatures range from 295 K (room/ambient)
     to ~500 K (heavy workload hotspot, ~225 °C), an MAE of ~3-5 K represents ~1.5-2.5%
     average relative temperature deviation.

B. ROOT MEAN SQUARED ERROR (RMSE):
   - Formula: RMSE = sqrt( (1 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N (T_pred,i - T_true,i)^2 )
   - Units: Kelvin [K].
   - Meaning: Quadratic mean of errors. Because errors are squared before averaging,
     RMSE penalizes large, localized errors far more severely than MAE.
   - Diagnosis: If RMSE is significantly larger than MAE (e.g., RMSE / MAE > 1.4), it
     reveals that the model has high localized errors (such as missing a peak hotspot),
     even if the majority of background cells have low error.

C. MEAN SIGNED ERROR (BIAS):
   - Formula: Bias = (1 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N (T_pred,i - T_true,i)
   - Units: Kelvin [K].
   - Meaning: Systematic tendency to either overheat or undercool the chip.
     - Positive Bias (> 0): The model systematically predicts higher temperatures than ground truth.
     - Negative Bias (< 0): The model systematically underpredicts temperatures (cool bias).
     - Zero Bias (= 0): Symmetrical errors with no directional drift.
   - Electronic engineering implication: A negative bias is dangerous in chip packaging,
     as it falsely reports that a chip is operating comfortably below its thermal throttling
     threshold when it is actually overheating.

D. COEFFICIENT OF DETERMINATION (R² SCORE):
   - Formula: R² = 1 - [ sum(T_true - T_pred)^2 / sum(T_true - mean(T_true))^2 ]
   - Dimensionless (range: (-inf, 1.0]).
   - Meaning: Proportion of temperature variance explained by the model compared to
     a trivial baseline that simply predicts the dataset mean everywhere.
     - R² = 1.0: Perfect prediction.
     - R² = 0.90 - 0.95: Strong engineering fidelity.
     - R² = 0.0: The model performs no better than predicting a constant mean temperature.
     - R² < 0.0: The model's predictions are worse than guessing the constant mean temperature.

E. MAXIMUM ABSOLUTE ERROR:
   - Formula: Max Error = max_{i} |T_pred,i - T_true,i|
   - Units: Kelvin [K].
   - Meaning: The worst single-pixel error across the entire validation dataset.
   - Why it matters: In high-performance electronic chips, thermal failure occurs at the
     worst-case hotspot, not at the average chip pixel. If a model has a low MAE of 3 K
     but misses a transistor cluster hotspot by 100 K, the chip will burn out in reality.

F. HOTSPOT LOCALIZATION DISTANCE:
   - Formula: dist = sqrt( (x_pred - x_true)^2 + (y_pred - y_true)^2 )
   - Units: Grid cells [cells] or physical distance [mm] (with dx = 0.3125 mm).
   - Meaning: The spatial offset between where the model thinks the hottest point on
     the chip is located versus where the true thermal hotspot actually is.
   - Engineering significance: In thermal management, heat pipes, thermoelectric coolers,
     and microfluidic channels must be placed directly over the hotspot. Missing the location
     by more than 2-3 cells (0.6 - 1.0 mm) means cooling the wrong silicon region.

G. PDE RESIDUAL (R_pde):
   - Formula: R_pde(x,y) = div(k(T) grad(T)) - (h_sink/d_chip)*(T - T_amb) + Q_vol
   - Physical Units: Watts per cubic meter [W/m³] or Megawatts per cubic meter [MW/m³].
   - Meaning: Net local energy conservation imbalance. For an exact physical solution,
     the heat generated by Joule dissipation plus heat entering by conduction must
     identically balance heat leaving by conduction and package convection (R_pde = 0).
   - Normalized Residual: Scaled by 1.0e6 (MW/m³) during training.
   - Critical Nuance: A small PDE residual DOES NOT automatically prove an accurate
     temperature prediction! For example, a trivial flat field T(x,y) = T_ambient with Q=0
     has zero residual but completely fails if there were active heat sources. Both data
     accuracy and physical consistency must be evaluated simultaneously.

H. BOUNDARY CONDITION ERRORS:
   - Dirichlet Error: |T_pred - T_boundary| [K] on edges where temperature is fixed.
   - Neumann (Insulated) Normal Gradient: |dT/dn| [K/m] across edges where no heat escapes.
   - Robin (Convective) Surface Flux: |-k dT/dn - h_edge*(T - T_amb)| [W/m²] on cooling edges.

4. FAIR BASELINE COMPARISON & RELATIVE IMPROVEMENT FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To fairly evaluate the PINN, it must be compared against the baseline FNO under identical
conditions:
  - Same dataset split: exactly the 800 held-out validation samples (no test set leakage).
  - Same architecture scale: 23 input channels, width=32, modes=8x8, 4 layers (~530,433 params).
  - Same target space: Kirchhoff transformation theta = psi(T), inverted analytically.
  - Same evaluation metrics: real temperature units in Kelvin.

The relative improvement of the PINN over the Baseline FNO is computed as:
    Improvement (%) = 100 * (Baseline_Error - PINN_Error) / Baseline_Error

Interpretation:
  - Positive Value (> 0%): PINN reduced the error (PINN is superior on this metric).
  - Negative Value (< 0%): Baseline FNO had lower error (Baseline is superior on this metric).
  - Zero (0%): Exactly identical performance.

Note: This formula is valid when Baseline_Error > 0. If Baseline_Error is 0, percentage
improvement is undefined.

WHY IS THE BASELINE FNO ACHIEVING LOWER DATA ERROR THAN THE PINN?
This is a standard research insight that students must be ready to explain:

1. THE "PHYSICS REGULARIZATION VS DATA FIDELITY" TENSION:
   In Physics-Informed Neural Networks, the PDE loss term:
       L_pde = mean( (R_pde / 1.0e6)^2 )
   involves the divergence of thermal flux:
       div(k grad theta)
   which computes spatial second-order derivatives (Laplacian-like smoothing).
   Second derivatives heavily penalize sharp curvatures, steep spatial gradients,
   and localized peaks.
   Because the loss function actively penalizes high curvature:
       L_total = 1.0 * L_data + 1e-4 * L_pde
   the optimizer is forced into a trade-off. To decrease the large PDE residual around
   sharp heat source boundaries, the network smooths out the peak temperatures!

2. GRADIENT DAMPING & NEGATIVE BIAS (-8.91 K):
   Because the PDE loss penalizes steep thermal gradients, the PINN acts like a spatial
   low-pass filter. It dampens the high-frequency thermal peaks created by small,
   concentrated heat sources. This creates a systematic negative bias (underpredicting
   the maximum temperatures by ~34.3 K on average).

3. PURE DATA FNO HAS NO CURVATURE RESTRICTIONS:
   The baseline FNO only optimizes L_data = MSE(pred, target). It is free to fit sharp,
   localized peaks directly from the training data without paying any mathematical penalty
   for high spatial curvature or flux divergence. Consequently, it achieves a lower MAE
   (3.65 K vs 10.57 K) and higher R² (0.95 vs 0.80).

4. ARE PINNs USELESS THEN? ABSOLUTELY NOT!
   This result illustrates that tuning loss weighting hyperparameters (w_pde, w_bc)
   is critical. In our model, w_pde = 1e-4 with a normalized residual scale of ~1e6
   contributed a substantial loss component (~0.18 vs data loss ~0.24), exerting significant
   regularization pressure. In future iterations, techniques such as:
     - Adaptive loss weighting (e.g., GradNorm, soft attention, or learning rate annealing)
     - Curriculum learning (starting with high w_data, gradually introducing w_pde)
     - Collocation point resampling near hotspots
   can mitigate this over-smoothing while retaining physical consistency.

7. COMMON WARNING SIGNS & DIAGNOSTIC GUIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Warning Sign 1: High Maximum Error (> 100 K) despite moderate MAE (~10 K)
  - Cause: Concentrated power density in small multi-core clusters produces intense, localized
    temperature spikes. The network predicts the smooth bulk temperature correctly but misses
    the apex.
  - Solution: Collocation point upsampling near heat sources; weighted MSE loss favoring high temperatures.

Warning Sign 2: Negative Bias (undercooling)
  - Cause: Regularization penalty on Laplacian flux divergence penalizes sharp peaks.
  - Solution: Rebalance w_pde / w_data loss ratio; normalize PDE residual by local heat source magnitude.

Warning Sign 3: Visually Smooth Predictions That Have High Physical Residual
  - Cause: A Fourier Neural Operator naturally represents functions as truncated Fourier series,
    which are infinitely differentiable (C-infinity smooth). However, physical electronic chips have
    sharp material discontinuities (dielectric k=1.4 W/mK adjacent to copper k=400 W/mK), where the true
    derivative is piecewise discontinuous, not smooth!
